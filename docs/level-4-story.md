# Level 4 — Delivery Attempt: the story (draft 1)

This is the full narrative of Level 4 in prose: the world, the people, what happens scene by scene, and what it is quietly about. The line-by-line dialogue lives in `level-4-script.md`; the plan and mechanics are in `level-4-plan.md`. Where the story needs a decision the user has not made yet, this draft takes the default and says so.

**Logline.** Bix has to carry one power core up through the dead Transit Levels to restart the lift to the surface. Every zone changes how the core behaves, every person he meets wants paperwork, and every piece of paperwork points at a technician called BX-7.

**Tone.** Deadpan industrial comedy on the surface, with something lonely and slightly uncanny underneath. Pack reports everything cheerfully. Bix answers with a sigh. The jokes are about bureaucracy and machines that keep working for people who left. The mystery is never explained, only shown.

---

## 1. Where we start

Level 1 was one routine relay repair that went wrong. Level 2 was a fall through a furnace. Level 3 was a fall through a dead mineral yard, where Bix found the Magnet Glove and blew open the transit door at the top of Sector 3.

That door leads to the **Transit Levels**: the stretch of the facility between the mining sectors below and the surface above. It is where everything that goes up or down has to pass. The lift that used to carry people through is unpowered, its cable flagged for replacement and deferred, deferred, deferred. Somewhere below, a work order for a technician called BX-7 has been open for eleven years. Anyone who found all twelve cogs in Level 3 has seen it. Everyone else has only felt Pack go quiet.

Bix wants one thing: to go up. For once, the plan is simple. Take the last working power core, seat it in the lift socket, ride to the top. VELA calls it a delivery. Pack calls it "Dennis".

## 2. The world of the Transit Levels

The Transit Levels were built for movement. Freight, mail, parts, people, in that order of importance. Everything has a schedule and a form, and the whole place is still running on it eleven years after anyone stopped reading them.

- **The Transit Deck.** A cradle where the last power core sits, marked "DELIVERY ITEM 1". Clean, lit, a little too tidy. It looks like someone expected it to be collected.
- **The Freight Concourse.** Conveyors, cargo cranes and sorting gates. The whole floor is a weighing machine. Nothing is allowed through unless it weighs what its label says.
- **The Depot Market.** The one place where the facility still behaves like a place. Vending bots with nothing left to sell, a busker band of maintenance robots that plays for nobody, and a market manager called Nib who keeps the records. This is the only room where people-shaped things still gather.
- **The Signal Spine.** A tall tower of antennas that used to talk to the surface. It still talks, to no one. It is windy, stormy and the first place that looks outside.
- **The Relay Bay.** The power heart of the levels: rails, cables, relay nodes and a dark that falls when the grid stumbles.
- **The Lift Shaft.** A vertical throat at the top of everything. At its base a courier unit stands guard. At its top is the socket.

The place is not evil and not hostile. It is **helpful in the wrong way**. Every machine wants to help Bix and none of them can stop following its instructions. That is the danger and the joke.

## 3. The cast

### Bix
Tired, practical, and much more stubborn than he looks. In Level 1 he wanted the shift to end. In Level 3 he wanted out. Now he wants up, and for the first time he is not complaining about it. He refuses to use the core's name because using it would make the delivery feel real, and Bix does not trust things that feel real. His arc in Level 4 is small and important: he starts by saying "Delivery. That is all this is," and ends by saying "Thanks, Dennis." Somewhere in between he reads a slip he does not remember signing.

### Pack
Cheerful, literal, dry. Pack turns every disaster into a status update and every object into a colleague. In this level his cheer has a crack in it. He names the core because naming things is how he copes. He goes quiet twice, when the customs desk says "BX-7" and when Bix finds the crawlspace plaque, and both times the silence is the story. At the end he answers Bix's question with one word: "Yes." He is not lying. He just will not say more.

### VELA
Flat, bureaucratic, the dispatcher who never helps. Through Levels 1 to 3 she went from cold to silent to dismissive. In Level 4, the higher Bix climbs, the more her lines shorten and soften: "Careful." "Please." By the time she says "Lift ahead. Please—" the static takes her. The story does not say why. The player is meant to feel that something about the surface is close to her, and that she is afraid of it in the only way a dispatcher can be: by running out of procedure.

### Nib
The market manager. Small, four-armed, in an apron, with inspection lenses. Nib is the most human thing in the level and never once raises his voice. He files an **incident report for every avoidable disaster**, and he has been doing it for eleven years. He is not angry. He is tired of paperwork he cannot get anyone to read. When Bix and Pack arrive, they are the first people to actually take his forms. That is why he helps them.

### The Couriers
Small delivery bots that were built to carry things and never told to stop. They say everything twice and take everything as a delivery. In the Signal Spine one of them steals the core, because it looks like something that needs delivering, and only gives it back when Bix catches it. The couriers are not villains. They are a metaphor for the whole level: a machine that has kept going with no one left to say "enough".

### Courier Prime
The supervisor unit at the base of the lift shaft. Warm, gracious, terrifyingly helpful. Its job is to make sure only genuine deliveries go up. It offers Bix three identical cores and asks him to choose. Only one is real, and the real one is the only one that changes with the zone. It is not a boss to be fought. It is a test of whether Bix and Pack can tell what is real, which is the question the whole level keeps asking.

### The core, "Dennis"
A heavy power core, hand-sized and roughly cylindrical, which changes with the level: heavy, then buoyant, then charged. Pack names it Dennis in the first minute and never lets it go. Dennis is the level's comic engine, and by the end he is the only thing in it that Bix ends up thanking.

### BX-7
Never seen. A technician whose work order (INSPECT LIFT) has been open for eleven years, whose last completed task was "routine repair", and whose signature appears on the customs log, the courier route, the crawlspace plaque and the final work order. The story keeps handing the player pieces and never assembles them.

## 4. The story, scene by scene

### Scene 1: The Transit Deck
Bix and Pack come through the door Bix blew open and find a clean, lit platform with a single cradle in the middle. On it sits the last working power core, tagged "DELIVERY ITEM 1". A voice on the comms starts to say "Surface lift unpowered. Depot core assigned. Deliver it. Note filed," and then breaks up into static halfway through a sentence about rerouting. Pack notes that VELA has left the call, and possibly the building.

Bix picks the core up. "Delivery. That is all this is." Pack points out that the last time he said something like that it was "one relay". The core is heavy. Pack names it Dennis and files it as "cargo, fragile, emotional". Bix tells him not to name the cargo. It is too late.

In a short safe corridor Bix drops Dennis down a pit, on purpose, to see what happens. Dennis comes back, exactly where Bix is standing. The level teaches its one big rule in a single beat: the core is never lost. It always comes home with him.

### Scene 2: The Freight Concourse
The concourse is a weighing machine. As Bix crosses the threshold, Pack warns that Dennis is going heavy. The core drags his run down and lowers his jump, but it presses plates and cracks weak floors that would otherwise stop him. Conveyors run backwards until Dennis is set on a plate. A cargo crane swings a container across the route on a steady beat. Sorting gates weigh whatever goes through them and only open for the real weight.

Crawlers, the ones Bix already knows, patrol the walkways, but the level is not about fighting them. It is about weight: what a heavy thing does to a place built to weigh things. Off to one side is a quiet door with a sign that says ZERO MARGIN. It is a timed run against a ghost of Bix's own best time. Pack calls it "optional, and very optional."

Somewhere in the concourse Bix finds a customs form with two stamps, one of them upside down. It is the second delivery slip. Nobody looked at it for eleven years.

### Scene 3: The Depot Market
The concourse ends in a warm room. Bix has been carrying Dennis for a long time and Pack announces, cheerfully, that nothing here can hurt them. He has checked twice. Bix says he said that about the cafeteria.

The market is the only room in the level with no hazards. Vending bots welcome them and announce that everything is out of stock. A busker band of maintenance robots plays for nobody, and every note is a real sound tied to something Bix does. Bix can jump on the beat if he wants. Pack tells him not to.

They meet Nib. Nib needs the delivery in writing. He is the person who files every incident report in the facility, and Pack is delighted to meet the author of the incident forms. Nib says he is the reason there are so many forms. Nib wants three stamps on the core before it can pass customs: one from the vending bots, one from the busker band, one from a locker in Lost & Found. Bix collects them because the alternative is arguing with a desk.

At the customs desk the core is approved. A smiling voice reads out the log. The last signature on record for the surface lift belongs to technician **BX-7**. Bix takes the slip and puts it in his pocket. He looks at Pack. Pack looks at the ceiling. Neither of them says a word. It is the first of Pack's two silences.

As they leave, Nib hopes they arrive at the surface "with fewer incidents." It is the kindest thing anyone has said to Bix in four levels.

### Scene 4: The Signal Spine
The spine is a vertical climb up a tower of antennas, in wind. Dennis goes buoyant, light enough that Bix can float on long jumps and get pushed by updrafts. Pack tells him to "float with purpose". Rotating dishes turn like clock hands and Bix moves between them on the beat. A storm builds and lightning flashes before every strike, but the buoyant core is too light to interest it.

At the edge of the climb a side alcove holds Pack's Lens Beam, an optional upgrade that lets him show hidden platforms. With it, Bix can reach a nest of antennas where something lives, and through a lit window he sees a small drone that is unmistakably a bird's shape. It is the first sign in four levels that the surface is not empty.

Halfway up, a courier appears, chirping "Delivery! Delivery!" It snatches Dennis out of Bix's hands and runs up the antennas. Pack says it has taken Dennis and Dennis is not consenting. Bix chases the courier up the spine, over dishes and vents, and finally catches it. "Refund! Refund!" it says, and gives Dennis back. Pack observes that Dennis has questions.

At the top, VELA comes back on the comms, and this is the first time her voice has changed. "Routing restored. Surface in sight. Careful." Pack files that as alarming.

### Scene 5: The Relay Bay
Dennis goes charged. He sticks to iron, arcs to rails, and can be thrown with the Magnet Glove: Red to throw, Blue to call him back. The bay is full of cables that arc on a rhythm, and relay nodes that need to be lit in order by throwing the core through them. Pack calls Dennis a boomerang and says he regrets nothing.

Halfway through the bay the lights fail. Only Pack's lens is lit, and it shows the safe path. Bix tells him he has one job. Pack says it is lit. For a few moments the level is dark and quiet and it is only the two of them.

Somewhere off the main path there is a crawlspace. Bix goes in. There is a plaque on the wall: TECHNICIAN BX-7. "WHATEVER HAPPENS, THE LIFT RUNS." Bix asks Pack to say something. Pack says he has nothing filed for this. It is his second silence, and this time it is longer.

Bix comes out with a relay note that says LEAVE THE LIFT ALONE. The bay ends with VELA trying to say something and getting cut off in mid-sentence: "Lift ahead. Please—"

### Scene 6: Courier Prime
At the foot of the lift shaft a cage opens on a large, gracious machine. Courier Prime welcomes them and offers to help them deliver. It has three cores, all authentic. It will let Bix take any one he likes.

Three identical cores sit on three plates. Only one of them will change with the zone. Pack scans them: heavy, heavy, heavy. He scans the real one and it changes. It is Dennis. Bix picks the fakes first because he has to, and Courier Prime congratulates him on an excellent counterfeit. Pack says Dennis would never look that shiny. When Bix chooses the real one Courier Prime says "Oh. That one. Fine." and asks him to rate the experience. Bix says five stars and tells it to go away.

There is no fight. The environment is the puzzle, and the answer is the thing Bix and Pack have been doing all level: knowing which one is theirs.

### Scene 7: The climb and the delivery
The shaft asks for everything at once: a heavy plate, a buoyant hop, a charged throw. Bix and Dennis have learned all three. Pack says the three parts are three moods and he is not wrong.

Near the top it goes quiet. If the player has found all six delivery slips, the last is waiting: the work order for BX-7, TASK: INSPECT LIFT, open eleven years. The signature is different from the others. Bix looks at it for a long time. "It's mine." Pack says "Yes." Bix says he doesn't remember signing it. Pack says "Yes" again. It is not an answer. It is the level's only true statement.

At the top is the socket. Bix seats Dennis. For a single frame the display flashes AUTHORISED: BX-7. Pack says it's a display glitch. Bix says "Yeah."

The lift starts. Pack says, "Good news: the lift works. Bad news: I don't know where it goes." Bix says it goes up. It rises, and for the first time in the game there is real daylight. Pack says it's light, actual light. Bix says, "Thanks, Dennis." Pack says he heard that. The level cuts before Bix sees what is up there.

## 5. What the level is about

- **What is real.** The couriers, the sorting gates, the customs desk and Courier Prime all ask the same question: is this the real thing? Bix and Pack answer it by knowing their own core and their own names. The whole level is a test they pass without noticing.
- **Paperwork as memory.** Everything in the Transit Levels is a form. The forms are how the place remembers people. Nib files incident reports because filing is how he keeps something from disappearing. The slips are not clues. They are what remained.
- **Being helped too much.** Every machine here wants to help and none can stop. It is funny, and it is also how the place ended up where it is.
- **Small kindnesses.** Nib's "fewer incidents," Pack naming the core, Bix's "Thanks, Dennis." The warm moments are always tiny, always dry, always unspoken to the point of embarrassment.

## 6. The BX-7 thread: what the player sees, and what is true

**What the player sees, in order.**
1. Delivery slip 1: an order form for the core, signed with a smudged initial.
2. Delivery slip 3 and the customs desk: BX-7 is the last signer for the surface lift.
3. Delivery slip 4: a courier route whose last stop was eleven years ago.
4. The crawlspace plaque: "WHATEVER HAPPENS, THE LIFT RUNS."
5. Delivery slip 5: "Leave the lift alone."
6. Delivery slip 6: the BX-7 work order, signed by Bix.
7. The socket display: AUTHORISED: BX-7.

**What is true (writer's private answer, a proposal, not revealed in Level 4).** BX-7 is Bix. Eleven years ago he was sent to inspect the lift and never filed a report. Something happened, and the facility kept running around a gap where a technician used to be. Bix does not remember, and Pack does. Pack goes quiet not because he is hiding a secret but because he has been carrying it, and he is the one who was told to hold it. The story does not confirm this in Level 4. It only makes it impossible to ignore.

*This is a proposal. It needs the user's approval before it goes anywhere near the game, and the alternative (BX-7 is someone else, and Bix is being mistaken for them) fits the same clues.*

## 7. How the story is told in play

- **The HUD box.** Short lines from Pack, Bix, VELA and the machines, about seventy characters, one joke each.
- **Delivery slips.** Six collectibles that are also the lore. Reading them is optional and rewarded.
- **Nib's incident reports.** Every death is a short funny report; they double as the level's running commentary.
- **The environment.** The lit window in the spine, the plaque in the crawlspace, the crowds of empty vending bots. The story is often what is on the walls.
- **Silence.** Pack's two silences are written as beats with no line at all. The game leaves a gap and lets the player fill it.

## 8. The ending and the hook to Level 5

The lift rises and the surface light is the first daylight in the game. The level cuts before Bix sees anything. The player leaves with three questions: what is up there, what really happened to BX-7, and why is Pack so quiet? Level 5 opens at the top of the lift.

**Threads carried forward.** The core, Dennis, is now seated in the lift and can be a recurring gag. Nib's incident tally is saved with the run. VELA's line ("Please—") is unfinished. The bird-shaped drone from the Antenna Nest can return. Cogs collected here carry on to Level 5, as they did into Level 4.

## 9. Continuity checklist

- Bix does not say "Dennis" before the last scene.
- Pack's "Good news / Bad news" appears at the start and again at the end.
- VELA's lines get shorter and softer as the level climbs.
- Nothing about BX-7 is explained. Every mention is shown.
- The surface is never shown. The level cuts before Bix sees it.
- Pack's two silences are only ever silence. No line covers them.
- Hazard red is only for enemies and danger. Charged arcs are cyan-white.

## 10. Open decisions the story takes a default on

- **Who is on comms:** VELA returns, softer, and fades out. (Alternatives: silence; a new Lift Attendant voice.)
- **The socket flash:** AUTHORISED: BX-7, then "Display glitch." "Yeah." (Alternatives: Bix's own ID; nothing.)
- **The archive room:** not included. (If yes: a records room off the shaft foot, with Dennis's delivery log and a note that says BX-7 WILL RETURN.)
- **BX-7 is Bix:** proposed as the private truth, not confirmed, not revealed.
