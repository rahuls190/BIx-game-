# Bix visual direction

This is a permanent production rule for every new image, sprite, effect, environment, UI illustration, and marketing visual made for Bix.

## Canonical reference

The canonical Pack design is `design/references/pack-character-v1.png`. Pack is Bix's transformable backpack companion: a compact rectangular industrial chassis with a large cyan camera lens, articulated utility arms, an antenna, worn off-white armor, charcoal machinery, and orange service panels.

Every future image must look as if it belongs beside this exact Pack and the approved Bix/Pack concept board. Do not redesign Pack as a round mascot, floating orb, cute toy, or generic robot.

## Required look

- Semi-realistic industrial hard-surface design with readable mechanical construction.
- Worn off-white armor, charcoal/dark steel structure, orange service panels, and restrained cyan light for Pack and safe technology.
- Hazard red belongs to enemies and immediate danger. Molten yellow/orange belongs to heat, furnaces, vents, and hot metal.
- Materials show practical wear: scratches, edge abrasion, grime, heat discoloration, fasteners, seams, vents, and service access.
- Bix and Pack retain the same proportions, wardrobe, lens language, color placement, and grounded near-future setting in every illustration.
- Silhouettes must remain clear at actual gameplay size. Animation poses must read without relying on captions.

## Do not drift

- No flat-vector, chibi, glossy toy, fantasy, steampunk, or clean white-laboratory redesigns.
- No unrelated blue-purple sci-fi palette or excessive neon.
- No baked backdrop on sprite plates. Use real transparency and keep each subject inside its assigned cell.
- No baked shadow or broad glow unless the effect itself is the asset; the game supplies runtime shadow and bloom.
- Do not overwrite released art. Increment the filename version and keep the authored master under `design/`.

## Delivery rule

Author large, ship at approximately twice the largest on-screen size. Validate RGBA sprite transparency, cell boundaries, visual scale, mobile readability, and animation order before connecting an image to the game.
